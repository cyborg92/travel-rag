# SkyTrail Airways Disruption and Irregular Operations Policy

> **Training notice:** This is a fictional policy created for a synthetic TravelAssist RAG dataset. It does not represent a real airline or legal entitlement.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy applies when SkyTrail Airways cancels a flight, significantly changes its schedule, causes a protected misconnection, or experiences an operational delay. It does not replace rights provided by applicable law.

## 2. Disruption categories

| Category | Definition for this policy |
|---|---|
| Minor delay | Arrival expected less than 2 hours late |
| Significant delay | Arrival expected 2 to 5 hours late |
| Major delay | Arrival expected more than 5 hours late |
| Cancellation | Scheduled flight will not operate |
| Protected misconnection | Passenger misses an onward flight on the same ticket because of an earlier disruption |
| Schedule change | Departure moves by at least 2 hours, arrival moves by at least 3 hours, or a nonstop journey gains a connection |

## 3. Passenger options

For a cancellation, major delay, protected misconnection, or qualifying schedule change, the passenger may choose one of the following:

1. **Rebooking:** Travel on the next reasonable SkyTrail Airways service to the ticketed destination, subject to availability.
2. **Later travel:** Move travel to another SkyTrail Airways flight within 7 days before or 14 days after the original date, subject to the same origin and destination.
3. **Refund:** Receive a refund for the unused affected sector and any unused sectors that no longer serve the original travel purpose.

Only one remedy may be selected for the same affected sector. Once a refund is completed, the passenger cannot also request free rebooking for that sector.

## 4. Rebooking rules

- No change fee or fare difference applies to an eligible involuntary rebooking.
- Rebooking should remain in the original cabin. If that cabin is unavailable, an agent may approve another cabin according to the recovery hierarchy.
- A change of origin or destination is not automatic. Nearby airports within 150 km may be considered with passenger agreement.
- Partner-airline rebooking requires an authorized agreement and must not be promised until confirmed.
- A passenger who voluntarily changes the protected itinerary again becomes subject to `ticket-change-policy.md`.

## 5. Care during controllable disruption

When a controllable disruption causes an extended wait, SkyTrail Airways may provide:

| Expected wait | Assistance |
|---|---|
| 2 to 4 hours | Meal or refreshment voucher |
| More than 4 hours | Additional meal support and reasonable communication access |
| Overnight | Hotel accommodation and airport-hotel transport when the passenger is away from home |

Care may be limited when local safety, immigration, or supplier availability prevents delivery. Passengers should retain itemized receipts when instructed to arrange reasonable alternatives themselves.

## 6. Events outside airline control

Events such as severe weather, air-traffic restrictions, airport closure, security events, or government action may limit accommodation and reimbursement. Rebooking or refund options still apply when required by this policy or applicable law, but additional expense reimbursement is not guaranteed.

## 7. Baggage during disruption

1. Checked baggage normally follows the rebooked itinerary.
2. Baggage retrieval during a short connection or same-day recovery cannot be guaranteed.
3. If a passenger accepts a refund and ends the journey, reasonable efforts will be made to return checked baggage at the stopping point.
4. Delayed baggage must be reported using the baggage-report reference process in `baggage-policy.md`.

## 8. Connections and separate tickets

A connection is protected only when all affected flights are issued on the same ticket or explicitly marked as protected. SkyTrail Airways is not responsible under this policy for an onward booking held on a separate ticket, although an agent may offer discretionary assistance.

## 9. Communication and confirmation

Operational information can change quickly. A proposed recovery option is not confirmed until the passenger accepts it and the system records the updated itinerary.

For a consequential rebooking action, the application must follow this sequence:

1. retrieve the booking and disruption details;
2. calculate eligible options;
3. present the exact itinerary and any additional conditions;
4. obtain explicit confirmation;
5. execute the stored option once;
6. return the updated ticket status and request reference.

## 10. Expense claims

Claims should include the booking reference, affected flight, disruption details, itemized receipts, and contact information. Duplicate claims for the same expense are not payable. Approval depends on cause, reasonableness, local rules, and whether assistance had already been offered.

## 11. Examples

### Example A: airline cancellation

A domestic flight is cancelled for an operational reason. The passenger may take the next available service, move travel within the allowed window, or request a refund of the unused journey.

### Example B: weather delay

A flight is delayed overnight because of severe weather. The passenger remains eligible for rebooking or refund as applicable, but hotel reimbursement is not guaranteed under this fictional policy.

### Example C: protected misconnection

The first flight on a single ticket arrives late and the passenger misses the onward flight. SkyTrail Airways rebooks the passenger to the next reasonable service without a change fee or fare difference.

### Example D: separate ticket

A passenger misses an independently booked onward flight on another airline. That onward flight is not protected by this policy.

## 12. Precedence and uncertainty

Applicable law, confirmed ticket conditions, and an approved disruption waiver take priority over this general document. When the available evidence does not establish eligibility, the assistant must state that evidence is insufficient and refer the case for agent review rather than inventing an entitlement.
